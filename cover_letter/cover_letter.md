Editor-in-Chief, General Relativity & Gravitation

Dear Editorial Board,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, “On The Gravitational Nature Of Time,” for publication in General Relativity and Gravitation.

The manuscript investigates whether the Lorentz factor can be represented as the slope of an additional, non-compactified spatial coordinate, $\gamma=\sqrt{1+(\partial_x y)^2}$, in a Kaluza–Klein-like construction. Treating the gradient of this coordinate as a pure-gauge four-potential, I show that the induced radial metric reproduces the spatial geometry of the Schwarzschild solution as a Flamm-type embedding, with the metric component $G_{44}$ fixed to unity and consistent with the Cassini bound on the post-Newtonian parameter. I further show that the relation $\beta^2=2Gm(R)/(c^2R)$ describes both the flow velocity in the Painlevé–Gullstrand form of Schwarzschild geometry and the Hubble flow of a flat universe, with opposite sign, and I place this result in the context of the established literature.

I want to be clear about the scope of the claims. Where the construction can be checked, it is equivalent to general relativity, and the temporal metric function is supplied by a postulate rather than derived. The manuscript states these limitations explicitly, together with the open problems: a field equation for the non-gradient potential, a dimensionally consistent treatment of the time-equivalent parameter, and a derivation of the temporal relation from the higher-dimensional geometry. I submit it as a geometric reformulation and a starting point for work that could yield predictions differing from general relativity, not as a finished alternative theory.

In the interest of transparency, I included several derivations that followed logically along my path to my final conclusion. While the logical path follows from first principles, the results are less than stellar producing results of $<0.5\%$ which correlates to $\sim 10\sigma$. I believe these specific derivations (section 2.3) show that these approximate symmetries are fundamental to the geometry and may be improved with further developed field equations, but I understand that their scientific validity is not on par with the second half of the paper as they rely on dilating electromagnetic variables in manner that is yet to be strictly defined, and I would be willing to forgo their inclusion to publish with your journal, if that is your reviewers decision.

I believe the manuscript will interest your readers working on higher-dimensional and embedding approaches to gravitation, and on the relationship between local gravitational flow and cosmological expansion.

This work is original, has not been published, and is not under consideration elsewhere. I have no competing interests to declare. I am an independent researcher with no institutional affiliation or funding of any kind.
