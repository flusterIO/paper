## Cover letter (paste into ScholarOne)

Dear Editors,

I submit for consideration in Physica Scripta the manuscript "Absolute Time in Higher-Dimensional Geometry: Classically Simultaneous Relativity via an Unwrapped Kaluza-Klein Dimension" (single author, Research Paper).

The manuscript asks whether the kinematics of special relativity, and the static clock rates of general relativity, can be written with a fourth spatial axis and no time dimension. It shows that they can, in a precise sense. A moving body traces a path through three-dimensional space and a non-compact density axis y. The inclination of that path carries the Lorentz factor (slope dy/dx = beta*gamma), the speed (its sine) and the rate of a clock carried along it (its cosine). Time readings are ratios of distances along the path, and the relativity of simultaneity becomes a displacement along y, so that every inertial observer can recover one absolute time. If space flows outward from a mass, as in the Hubble flow, the space around the mass is inclined into y in the same way, and for steady fields the time coordinate can be eliminated from the motion of light and matter. The resulting flat-space paths reproduce the Schwarzschild orbit equation, light bending, the innermost stable circular orbit and the perihelion advance. These results are verified symbolically and numerically in an open-source package.

I want to be explicit about scope. Where the construction has been developed it is equivalent to special and general relativity, and its gravitational results rely on field equations imported from general relativity. The manuscript therefore presents these results as consistency checks, not new predictions, and it states in a table what is an identity of the geometry, what is recovered from general relativity, what is assumed, and what is not established. Its contribution is the unification of the Lorentz factor, speed, clock rate and synchronization offset as one geometric quantity, the account of time that follows from it, and an identification of where an independent dynamics for y could make the model differ from general relativity. The work is related to Euclidean and neo-Lorentzian formulations of relativity, induced-matter Kaluza-Klein theory, and the Zermelo/Randers and Jacobi-metric treatments of motion, and the manuscript discusses these.

I believe the paper suits Physica Scripta's remit of original theoretical work across physics, and its foundations-of-relativity readership in particular. The manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere. I have no competing interests and no funding to declare. The code that reproduces and checks every result is available as the alpha-omega-gravity package.

Thank you for your consideration.
